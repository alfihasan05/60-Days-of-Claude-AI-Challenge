# 1. Executive Summary

For Day 24, I took the customer and MVP insights from Day 23 and looked at the startup from a **business perspective**.

Instead of only asking:

> "What should I build?"

I focused on:

> "Can this become a sustainable business?"

The analysis focused on:

- Revenue
- Pricing
- Customer acquisition
- Distribution
- Retention
- Competition
- Business risks
- Competitive advantage
- Investment readiness

The biggest lesson was that a good MVP does not automatically create a good business.

A sustainable startup needs to prove:

**Customers want it → Customers pay → Customers stay → Customers can be acquired profitably**

### Final Verdict

The next priority is getting **real customers, real payments, and real retention data**.

# 4. Business Reality Check

## Who Pays?

The most important question is:

> **Who actually controls the budget for this problem?**

The target customer needs to be defined by more than demographics.

Important questions:

- Who experiences the problem?
- Who uses the product?
- Who makes the buying decision?
- Who controls the budget?
- How frequently does the problem occur?
- What does the problem currently cost them?

---

## Why Would They Pay?

Customers pay for outcomes, not features.

Potential value includes:

- Saving time
- Saving money
- Increasing productivity
- Increasing revenue
- Reducing risk
- Removing repetitive work
- Improving decisions
- Making workflows easier

---

## How Will Customers Discover It?

Potential acquisition channels:

1. Direct outreach
2. LinkedIn content
3. Online communities
4. Referrals
5. Partnerships
6. Educational content
7. Organic search
8. Paid acquisition

The initial strategy should focus on **one primary acquisition channel** instead of trying everything at once.

# 5. Biggest Growth Risks

### 1. Distribution Risk

The product may work but fail to reach enough potential customers.

### 2. Problem Severity Risk

Customers may like the idea without considering it important enough to purchase.

### 3. Retention Risk

Users may try the product once and never return.

### 4. Pricing Risk

The proposed price may not match customer-perceived value.

### 5. Competition Risk

Existing alternatives may already solve enough of the problem.

### 6. Acquisition Cost Risk

The business may spend too much acquiring customers.

# 8. Go-To-Market Strategy

## Phase 1 — Founder-Led Sales

The founder should personally:

- Find potential customers
- Contact them
- Demonstrate the MVP
- Onboard them
- Observe their behavior
- Ask for payment
- Collect objections
- Improve the product

This creates a fast customer feedback loop.

## Phase 2 — Focused Distribution

Use:

**Specific Customer → Specific Problem → Specific Message → Specific Offer**

Avoid:

**Everyone → Everything → Generic Marketing**

---

## Phase 3 — Repeatable Growth

Once one channel consistently produces customers:

**Document → Optimize → Automate → Delegate → Scale**
