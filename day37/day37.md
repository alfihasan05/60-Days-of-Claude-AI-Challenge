 Day 37 — Task Compass
 Learn How Work Flows Through Real Organizations

Task Compass is an interactive organizational simulation designed to build practical workplace decision-making skills.

Instead of testing knowledge of job titles, the experience focuses on understanding how work moves between people, teams, and departments.

---

 What I Built

I created **Task Compass**, a single-file offline web application using:

- HTML
- CSS
- Vanilla JavaScript
- Drag-and-drop interactions
- Animated workflow transitions
- Interactive scoring
- Responsive dark UI
- Organizational Thinking Dashboard
- Final reflection system

No React, Tailwind, npm, Babel, external libraries, APIs, or backend services were used.

 Three-Stage Simulation
 Stage 1 — Who Owns This?

I worked through realistic workplace tasks and identified the most appropriate primary owner.

The application explains:
- Primary Owner
- Why they own the task
- Supporting roles
- The reasoning behind the decision
This helped reinforce the difference between **ownership and contribution**.

 Stage 2 — Task Routing

I built workflows by arranging roles in the order that work would typically move through an organization.

This stage focused on:

- Handoffs
- Dependencies
- Workflow sequence
- Cross-functional communication

Stage 3 — Collaboration Challenge

Complex workplace situations required assigning multiple teams instead of selecting a single owner.

The simulation evaluates:

- Primary Owner
- Supporting Teams
- Reasoning
- Communication Flow

This demonstrated how larger organizational problems often require coordinated teamwork.

## Organizational Thinking Dashboard

The final dashboard evaluates understanding across four areas:

| Category | Focus |
|---|---|
| Ownership | Identifying primary responsibility |
| Delegation | Knowing when work should move to another role |
| Collaboration | Recognizing cross-functional dependencies |
| Workflow Thinking | Understanding how work moves through an organization |

---

## Key Learnings

### Ownership ≠ Doing Everything

A person can own an outcome while depending on several other people to deliver it.

### Delegation Improves Workflow

Effective teams distribute work based on responsibility and expertise rather than expecting one person to handle everything.

### Complex Problems Need Collaboration

Many workplace problems cross departmental boundaries, making communication and coordination essential.

### Workflow Thinking Matters

Understanding the sequence of work can be just as important as knowing who performs each task.

### Communication Enables Handoffs

A successful handoff requires more than passing a task. Context, expectations, status, and feedback also need to move with the work.

---

## Biggest Insight

> Organizations are not simply collections of job titles. They are interconnected systems of ownership, collaboration, delegation, communication, and workflows.

Task Compass helped me think beyond:

**"Who does this task?"**

and start thinking:

**"Who owns the outcome, who contributes, what happens next, and how should the work flow?"**
